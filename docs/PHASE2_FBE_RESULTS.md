Even unfinished, it shows the scaling: JSON in MQL is unusable for bulk payloads (deals history).

## Layer 2 — MQL end-to-end round trip (MQL encode → DLL → FinCore → typed echo → DLL → MQL decode)

### S — the real `SIGNAL_BACKTEST_DECISION` path on the live FinCore (2026-11-10, 14:45)

FinCore was running for real: the operator's instance, with MT5 attached, the decision engine up and
`BenchEcho` off. `mqlc_bench` (the DLL's `Session`, i.e. `DoSendMessage` / `DoSendBinary` without the
MQL array copy) sent the S fixtures:
- JSON `S_request.json` to port 2022;
- FBE `Hello` + `S_request.bin` to port 2024, each with a fresh `ReqId`.

Both requests are the same decision request (EURUSD, `default`, as-of 2026-01-01). The first request

## Verdict (provisional)

Where the time goes: the hypothesis holds. For everything above a trivial payload, the JSON
encode/decode at both ends dominates the round trip, and MQL's `CJAVal` dominates by far. One 1039
decision costs about 50 µs of `CJAVal` work in MQL against about 85 µs for the whole DLL loopback
round trip. 20 orders cost about 0.7 ms in MQL against 0.1 ms on the wire, and 1 000 deals do not
finish in usable time. FBE removes 98–99 % of the codec cost on both sides (MQL 47–111×, C#
12–91×), cuts C# allocations by 95–100 %, and halves the bytes on the wire. The DLL itself is
neutral for small frames and 4.5× faster for large ones.

The live 1039 measurement changes the picture for **backtesting**. A cached decision costs about
0.8 ms end to end, and about 0.7 ms of that is FinCore's handler, not encoding. FBE makes one decision
only **~1.1× faster as MQL sees it** (about 70–110 µs saved). It does make the tail much tighter: p99 is
1.5–3× lower. A Strategy Tester run therefore cannot get ≥ 20 % faster from FBE unless the decision
round trip is most of the tester's time *and* the handler gets faster too.

**Go/no-go (per criterion):**
- *Tester ≥ 20 % faster:* **not expected** (the decision itself is only ~10 % faster; pending a real run).
- *MQL end-to-end p50 on M ≥ 2×:* **passes**: 3.6× measured on the live DLL + FinCore path, about 9.7× with
  MQL `CJAVal` encode/decode included. L: 12× (DLL + FinCore). **Go for the bulk signals.**

**Correction (found while moving the bulk signals):** production MQL does **not** build
`SIGNAL_DEALS_HISTORY` / `SIGNAL_ACTIVE_ORDERS` with `CJAVal`. `CAccountSnapshot` concatenates the JSON
as strings, so the layer-1 MQL figures (measured against `CJAVal`) overstate the MQL-side gain for
these two signals. The C# side's gain stands: `Utils.ExtractList<DealInfo>` takes 10.3 ms and 10 MB
per 1 000 deals, against 0.12 ms for FBE. Two further facts:
- Both are background sync messages from `TerminalsSyncJob`, not trading-latency messages.
- The DLL holds **one process-wide connection with one `Protocol`**. Sending them over FBE therefore
  means switching the master EA's whole connection to FBE: every other signal as
  `GenericSignal{DataJson}`, and the server's pushes multicast on the FBE listener as well.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Adopt FBE for the bulk signals.** These are `SIGNAL_DEALS_HISTORY`, `SIGNAL_ACTIVE_ORDERS` and any
   other list payload. There the gain is large and measured on both sides (MQL 88× on M; C# 51–91×
   decode; DLL 4.5× on L), and JSON with `CJAVal` breaks down on 1 000 deals. The schemas
   (`DealsHistory`, `ActiveOrders`) already exist.
2. **Keep 1039 on FBE (it is already done, with a small gain and a tighter tail).** But to speed up
   backtests, profile `BacktestSignalHandler` next: its ~700 µs per cached decision is now the
   bottleneck (the async hand-off, the cache lookup, and Serilog per request). A short-circuit for
   cache hits on the session thread is the likely win.
3. **Leave the other ~30 small signals on JSON** (or carry them through `GenericSignal{DataJson}`) until a
   profile shows them. For small payloads the wire and handler dominate, not the codec.
